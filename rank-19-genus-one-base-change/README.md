# A genus-one family with rank at least 19

**Status:** Draft for independent review

**Subject:** Elliptic curves over $\mathbb Q$ with trivial torsion

**Date:** 24 October 2026

## The result

We found a pair of quadratic covers of a rank-17 elliptic surface. Each cover
adds a rational section. Taken together, the covers give **19 independent
sections** over the function field of a genus-one curve. That parameter curve
has a rational point of infinite order, so it produces infinitely many
rational specializations. We also identify infinitely many specializations
with trivial torsion.

In particular, this construction gives infinitely many elliptic curves over
$\mathbb Q$ with trivial torsion and rank at least 19. “At least” matters:
we have not proved that the generic rank, or the rank of any particular
specialization, is exactly 19.

This does **not** raise the known bound in [Dujella's $C(0)$ table](https://dujella.github.io/tors/generic.html),
which already lists 19. We have not determined whether our pair of covers is
equivalent to the one in [Elkies's 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25406v1).
We offer the construction and its exact data for review on those terms.

## The construction

The parent surface has 17 independent sections. Its Weierstrass equation
and their coordinates are included in [`data/parent-rank17.json`](data/parent-rank17.json).
The two covers of its parameter line are

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_1^2 &= 30015061720809t^2-20692173881368t+3538696407184,\\
r_2^2 &= 46784549960425t^2-29557331565600t+4702049024256.
\end{aligned}
$$

Their branch points are distinct, so the common parameter curve has genus
one. At $t=15122/33609$, both right-hand sides are rational squares;
this supplies a rational point on the curve. On its elliptic model, that
point has infinite order: the group orders at the good primes 31 and 37
are 30 and 32, while twice the point is nonzero.

Each cover adds one explicit section to the inherited 17. Exact height
pairings give a positive definite $18\times18$ matrix for each cover, both
with determinant $452984832$. The two added sections change under different
cover involutions. Their differences from their conjugates each have
positive height 12. Applying the two involutions to any proposed relation
shows that neither new section can be generated by the other 18. Thus the
combined family has rank at least 19 over its genus-one function field.

The seed fiber has group orders 42, 46, and 71 modulo 31, 37, and 59.
Their greatest common divisor is one, proving trivial rational torsion.
The points $(1+1440k)P$, for integers $k$, give infinitely many fibers
with the same reductions. The specialization theorem retains
the 19 independent sections for all but finitely many of them.

## Proof and exact data

The [technical note](PROOF.md) gives the equations, the positive-rank and
torsion arguments, and the independence proof in full. The four JSON files
in [`data/`](data/) contain the parent model, both lifted models, all 53
section coordinates, the parameter curve, and the height matrices.

To check the packet with SageMath, run from this directory:

```bash
sage -python verify.py
```

The [verifier](verify.py) recomputes the section identities, height
pairings, cover maps, deck actions, and finite-field group orders from the
included exact data. It uses no Rank Hunter database or plugin.
